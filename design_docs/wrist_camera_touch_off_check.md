# Wrist Camera Touch Off Check

## What it does, at a glance

[`Check Wrist Camera By Touch-Off`](../src/picknik_009_ur5e_hw_config/objectives/check_wrist_camera_by_touch_off.xml) (HW config, `3. Calibration Routine`) measures how far the wrist camera's pick points land from reality, through the same conversion the pipeline uses. Each sample is: teleop to a viewpoint, snapshot the wrist camera, click a feature in the image, teleop the suction cup tip onto that feature. The clicked pixel goes through `GetPoseFromPixelCoords` exactly as a Gemini pick point does (normalized colour pixel, colour `camera_info`, same neighbour radius), so the estimate is what a grasp would have aimed at. The estimate, `grasp_link` at the snapshot and `grasp_link` at the touch are saved in world to `objectives/calibration/wrist_touch_off_samples.yaml`, one set of sections per sample. [`scripts/wrist_touch_off_report.py`](../scripts/wrist_touch_off_report.py) (host side, no ROS) prints each sample's error and fits one rigid correction of the camera pose in `grasp_link`.

[`Test Wrist Camera Calibration`](../src/picknik_009_ur5e_hw_config/objectives/test_wrist_camera_calibration.xml) (same config and subcategory) is the quick visual counterpart: the same snapshot and click, but the arm then drives the cup to the clicked point itself and stops a short way in front of it, so the miss can be seen directly. It repeats until stopped and saves nothing.

## Why this shape

- **It tests the pick chain, not the camera alone.** The estimate passes through the arm's forward kinematics (FK) at the snapshot, the camera mount, the D415's depth-to-colour registration and the cloud. The touch passes through FK at the touch pose and the EPick dimensions. A board calibration checks a subset of that; this checks the whole path a grasp depends on.
- **Every sample is from its own arm pose.** One pose cannot tell a mount error from the FK error at that pose. A mount tuned on a single touch-off nulls both there, and the error returns elsewhere. Several poses let the report fit a mount correction and then check, by leave-one-out, whether one fixed correction explains them.
- **The error is split along and across the cup axis.** The grasp descends along the cup axis until contact, so error along it is absorbed. Error across it is what moves the cup off the feature. The along part also contains where the cup lip sits relative to `grasp_link` and how firmly the operator touched, so it is not a camera measurement on its own.
- **Samples are saved, fitting is offline.** A Behavior Tree cannot do a least-squares fit, and the samples are worth keeping across stack restarts and calibration changes. `SavePoseStampedToYaml` with `overwrite` false appends each section and keeps the rest of the file.

## Notes and gotchas

- **`Test Wrist Camera Calibration` is for comparing mounts by eye, not for fitting one.** It goes from the clicked point to the hover pose by the same path a pick takes: the surface normal is turned into the approach axis as the unshelve collection does, and `PlanApproachTrajectory` plans the straight-line approach. A sideways miss at the hover pose is the error that moves a grasp off its target. A gap that is larger or smaller than the hover offset is depth error, which a real grasp absorbs on the way down. To compare two mounts, apply one, restart, click the same features from the same viewpoints, then do the same with the other (see the candidate sections in [`board_based_calibration.md`](board_based_calibration.md)). The cup stops short of the surface so that a depth error up to the offset leaves a gap instead of pressing into the feature. An error larger than that collides, so start with the arm slow. The approach is collision-checked only against the planning scene, not the wrist cloud. When the numbers matter, use the touch-off check and the report.

- **The arm's own kinematics are loaded on hardware, and they are a small part of the error.** The xacro used to load `ur_description`'s nominal `default_kinematics.yaml`, and the UR driver logged at every start that the connected robot's calibration did not match. The hardware config now sets the xacro's `use_calibrated_kinematics`, which loads [`config/ur5e_009_kinematics.yaml`](../src/picknik_009_ur5e_config/config/ur5e_009_kinematics.yaml), extracted from the controller with `ur_calibration`'s `calibration_correction` launch (read-only on the robot; `ur_calibration` is not in the drivers image, so it has to be installed first). Sim and mock keep the nominal values. At this cell's waypoints the factory kinematics move the cup tip by a few millimetres and the camera by a fraction of a degree, much less than the first touch-off set's pose-dependent error, so the arm kinematics alone did not explain it. Re-extract the file if the arm is re-calibrated or replaced. A leave-one-out error well above the fitted residual still means the error is not one fixed mount offset.
- **The wrist D415's depth-to-colour rotation is not modelled.** The camera reports about half a degree between its depth and colour sensors (`/camera/wrist_mounted_camera/extrinsics/depth_to_color`), while the URDF has the two frames parallel. Projected at shelf distance that is a few millimetres across the cup axis, and a fitted correction partly absorbs it.
- **The snapshot cloud is shown at its own stamp's transform** (`use_latest_transform` false), so it stays put while the arm moves for the touch. A cloud shown at the latest transform would move with the arm and make the comparison meaningless.
- **Touch lightly.** Pressing compresses the cup's bellows and moves `grasp_link` past the surface along the cup axis. That only changes the along part, but it shifts the fitted translation.
- **A re-run adds samples; it does not overwrite.** Sections are `<sample_prefix>_<n>_<part>`. At start the Objective probes the file for `<prefix>_<n>_error_world`, the last section a finished sample writes, and numbers new samples after the last one found. Probing is needed because the UI does not pass Objective inputs, so `sample_prefix` cannot be changed per run. A sample interrupted before its error section is written is not counted, so its partial sections are overwritten by the next run's sample of the same number, which is what you want. To start a separate set, change `sample_prefix` (and pass the same `--prefix` to the report). Every sample is saved when its touch finishes, so aborting loses only the one in progress.
- **Applying the fit is a separate step.** The report gives X in `grasp_link` (`camera_new = X * camera`). Turning that into the `wrist_camera_mount` section of [`cell_calibration.yaml`](../src/picknik_009_ur5e_config/objectives/calibration/cell_calibration.yaml), which is relative to `d415_mount_link`, needs the URDF chain between the two frames. Once applied, a rebuild-free stack restart picks it up (see [`board_based_calibration.md`](board_based_calibration.md)).
