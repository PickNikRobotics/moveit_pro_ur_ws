# Camera Naming

## Names, frames, and topics are all unified

Two cameras participate in the picking pipeline: the bin-viewing camera (mounted on top of the calibrated pole, looking down into the bin) and the wrist-mounted camera. Both **camera names** and **TF frame names** are identical between sim and HW:

| Camera | Name (MJCF + URDF + frame_id) | Optical frame |
|---|---|---|
| Bin camera | `scene_camera` | `scene_camera_optical_frame` |
| Wrist camera | `wrist_mounted_camera` | `wrist_mounted_camera_color_optical_frame` |

A third camera, `shelf_cam`, is a Stereolabs ZED 2i and is not a picking-pipeline input today. The sim carries it too, as one MJCF camera named `shelf_cam_left_camera` so that its image frame is the hardware's left optical frame. It still follows this page: same `/camera/<name>/...` topic layout and suffixes, same payload types. See [`zed_shelf_cam.md`](zed_shelf_cam.md).

This means a BT node, C++ behavior, or YAML that talks in **frame_ids** is portable across sim and HW out of the box. That was not true historically — the sim bin camera was named `tote_camera` and an unrelated overhead camera held the `scene_camera` name.

**ROS topic names are also unified.** Sim and HW publish each logical stream on the same topic, namespaced under `/camera/<camera_name>/...` with RealSense-driver suffixes (`color/image_raw`, `depth/image_rect_raw`, `depth/color/points`, `color/camera_info`). On HW the RealSense driver produces this layout natively; in sim, `picknik_mujoco_ros::apply_camera_sensor_overrides` reads per-camera `<sensor>` blocks from `ur_mujoco_ros2_control.xacro` (one per camera, with `rgb_topic` / `depth_topic` / `points_topic` / `camera_info_topic` `<param>` children) and rewrites the publisher names to match. See moveit_pro PR #18783 for the override mechanism.

## HW/Sim switching subtrees

Even though the topics now match, the workspace still routes camera reads through the HW/Sim-switching subtree pattern (the `6. HW/Sim Switching Subtrees` Objective subcategory):

- `Get Bin Image` → wraps `GetImage` + `GetCameraInfo` on the bin camera.
- `Get Bin Depth Image` → wraps `GetImage` on the bin camera's depth stream.
- `Get Bin Point Cloud` → wraps `GetPointCloud` on the bin camera.
- `Get Wrist Image` → wraps `GetImage` + `GetCameraInfo` on the wrist camera.
- `Get Wrist Point Cloud` → wraps `GetPointCloud` on the wrist camera.

The variants now share identical topic strings, so the indirection is no longer load-bearing for topic resolution — but it remains the canonical entry point for camera reads and absorbs any future sim/HW divergence (timeouts, framerate, optional message kinds) without rippling into callers.

**Rule of thumb for new BT code:** never write `topic_name="/camera/scene_camera/..."` directly in an objective that's meant to be sim-or-HW portable. Call the appropriate switching subtree instead. Direct topic literals are fine inside the switching-subtree variants themselves and inside experimental sim-only or HW-only objectives.

## Where the camera names are defined

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| `picknik_009_ur5e_config_sim/templates/calibrated_scene.xml.in` (installed as the generated `mjcf/assets/calibrated_scene.xml`) and `mjcf/assets/additions.xml` | MJCF `<camera>` and `<site>` definitions for sim. Names: `scene_camera`, `scene_camera_optical_frame`, `shelf_cam_left_camera`, `shelf_cam_left_camera_optical_frame`, plus the unrelated overview viewpoint `overview_camera` / `overview_camera_optical_frame`. |
| `picknik_009_ur5e_config_sim/mjcf/assets/robot_description.urdf` | Sim wrist camera (`wrist_mounted_camera`) and its frame chain. |
| `picknik_009_ur5e_config/description/scene_camera.urdf` | HW-side static D415 mesh placement for the bin camera (`scene_camera_link`). |
| `picknik_009_ur5e_hw_config/launch/cameras.launch.xml` | HW driver invocation for all three cameras: the two RealSenses (`scene_camera`, `wrist_mounted_camera`) and the ZED (`shelf_cam`), with their serial numbers and the `calibration_mode` switch. Also the wrist camera's color-grid `aligned_points` cloud (see Notes). |
| `picknik_009_ur5e_hw_config/launch/pro_zed_launch.py` | Maps the ZED's native topic tree onto the `/camera/shelf_cam/...` layout. |

`picknik_mujoco_ros::cameras.cpp` derives the published topic names directly from the MJCF camera `name` attribute (`<name>/color`, etc.); no separate sim-side topic config exists.

## Notes / gotchas

- **The wrist camera is currently disabled in sim.** Both the MJCF `<camera name="wrist_mounted_camera">` element in `robot_description.xml` and the matching `<sensor>` override block in `ur_mujoco_ros2_control.xacro` are commented out (paired — re-enable both together). The TF link/joint chain for the wrist camera in `robot_description.urdf` is intentionally left in place so collision and frame transforms still resolve. While disabled, sim runs of `Get Wrist Image` / `Get Wrist Point Cloud` will time out — they're HW-only for now.
- **Sim fixed cameras sit at the calibrated poses.** The sim package build writes the MJCF bodies `calibrated_camera` and `calibrated_shelf_cam` from the camera sections of `cell_calibration.yaml`, and the MuJoCo plugin publishes each camera's optical site straight from the model, so a sim image and its TF always agree. `Load Calibrated Scene` publishes `world -> scene_camera_link` and `world -> shelf_cam_camera_link` from the same sections. The two agree after the sim package is rebuilt; until then a recalibrated pose shows in the planning scene but not in the rendered camera.
- **Sim camera frame sizes follow one shared offscreen buffer.** The plugin requires every camera's resolution to equal the buffer, so the sim ZED renders the same 4:3 frame as the sim D415. Its vertical field of view is chosen so its focal length equals the real ZED's grab mode; the real camera's 16:9 frame and downscaled publishing are not reproduced.
- **`overview_camera` is sim-only and unused as a perception input.** It exists as a fixed external rendering viewpoint for the MuJoCo offscreen render and has no HW counterpart. It is `RGB_ONLY` (MJCF `<camera ... user="1" />`, gated by `<size nuser_cam="1" />` at the model root), so picknik_mujoco_ros skips depth and point-cloud rendering for it; the `<sensor>` override block only sets `rgb_topic` and `camera_info_topic`. No BT subscribes to it. If you find yourself reaching for it in code, you probably want `scene_camera` instead.
- **The C++ `GeminiResponseStep` default frame** (`kLoadFrameId` in `shelf_stocking_behaviors/src/gemini_response_step.cpp`) is `scene_camera_optical_frame`. This is correct for both sim and HW; do not branch on environment.
- **`detected_points.yaml`** (test data for the Gemini load path) lives under `picknik_009_ur5e_config_sim/objectives/` and uses `frame_id: scene_camera_optical_frame`. Same answer is correct for HW replays of saved data.
- **HW optical frames carry the stream name.** On hardware the RealSense driver names the frames `<camera>_color_optical_frame` and `<camera>_depth_optical_frame` (seen in a TF snapshot on the Agent PC). The table's `scene_camera_optical_frame` is the sim MJCF site name. Calibration code that converts to `<camera>_link` must use the image's own frame: see [`board_based_calibration.md`](board_based_calibration.md).
- **The HW point cloud is organized on the depth grid, not the color grid.** `depth/color/points` is in `<camera>_depth_optical_frame`, and its row and column follow the depth camera's intrinsics, even with `align_depth.enable` on. A color-image pixel therefore does not index the cloud directly. Pass the color `camera_info` to any behavior that maps image pixels into the cloud (`GetPoseFromPixelCoords`, `GetMasks3DFromMasks2D`). See [`gemini_point_detection.md`](../src/shelf_stocking/design_docs/gemini_point_detection.md). In sim, one MuJoCo camera renders both, so the two grids coincide and code that indexes the cloud by pixel looks correct there.
- **The wrist camera also has a cloud on the color grid, `aligned_points` (HW only).** GraspScout's seal check indexes the cloud by color pixel and accepts any cloud of the image's width and height, so the depth-grid `depth/color/points` would pass its size check and silently test the wrong surface. `cameras.launch.xml` therefore runs a `depth_image_proc` `point_cloud_xyz_node` on the driver's `aligned_depth_to_color` stream, which publishes `/camera/wrist_mounted_camera/aligned_points` in `wrist_mounted_camera_color_optical_frame`, organized like the color image, at the depth rate. It depends on `align_depth.enable`. The GraspScout objectives read it and fall back to image-only, with no seal check, when it is missing; that fallback logs `No aligned wrist cloud on ...` as a warning, so a run that logs it is not seal-checked. Only GraspScout uses it: `GetPoseFromPixelCoords` keeps using `depth/color/points` with the color `camera_info`. Pixels with no depth reading can come through at the 16-bit maximum (about 65 m) instead of as holes; they are far behind any product. The scene camera has no such node, and sim does not need one because its grids already coincide.
- **`Get Wrist Pointcloud`** (HW config, `7. Utilities`) is the runnable check for the wrist camera. It calls `Get Wrist Point Cloud`, clears the previous snapshot, and shows the cloud in the MoveIt Pro 3D Visualizer. The switching subtrees themselves only return the cloud, and their `point_cloud` port must stay the `{point_cloud}` blackboard key: it is the output, and putting a topic name there instead leaves every caller without a cloud.
- **Read a RealSense serial from the driver log**, never from `lsusb`: `lsusb` prints a different USB serial string.
- **The scene camera runs the RealSense spatial filter with bounded hole filling (`cameras.launch.xml`).** Shiny flat tops, the tuna lid in particular, lose depth under the D415's projector. When a Gemini point lands in such a hole, `GetPoseFromPixelCoords` finds no cloud point within its fixed pixel tolerance and fails, and because it converts every Gemini point in one call, before a product is chosen, one bad point ends the whole perception cycle rather than just that product. The hole's size changes from frame to frame, so the failure comes and goes. The spatial filter's `holes_fill` fills only holes up to a set size. `hole_filling_filter` was not used because it fills every hole, including the shadows beside products, with a neighbour's depth, which would widen product fits. `pro_rs_launch.py` adds `spatial_filter.holes_fill` as a launch argument because upstream exposes only `spatial_filter.enable`; its default is the driver's own (no filling), so the wrist camera is unchanged. The filter also smooths depth slightly everywhere. The Trainer records only the colour image, so datasets are unaffected. Filling only makes a point exist: the depth on a shiny lid can be a reflection several centimetres behind the real surface, with or without the filter, so a pick point on such a lid may be too deep.
- **The RealSense nodes do not hardware-reset the cameras on open (`initial_reset` is false in `cameras.launch.xml`).** The drivers container's `/dev` is a snapshot taken when the container starts. After a reset, a camera re-enumerates with new `/dev/video*` and `/dev/bus/usb` numbers, and the node only finds it again if those numbers happen to match the old ones. With two D415s resetting at once that is a race. When it is lost, the node logs `device with serial number ... is NOT found. Will Try again` forever, while the kernel log shows the camera re-enumerating normally. The same applies to any replug during a session: the camera stays unreachable until the drivers container restarts. Turning the reset off gives up its one benefit, clearing a camera wedged by a UVC "Protocol error" on restart; that now takes a replug followed by a drivers restart.
- **Check both D415s enumerate at USB 3.** The driver warns `connected using a 2.1 port` at startup, and `lsusb -t` shows the speed. At USB 2 the scene camera's 30 Hz color frames still carry even header stamps but reach the recorder in bursts. Trainer conversion times frames by arrival, so the converted dataset stutters (see [`cracker_box_pick_drop_collection.md`](../src/shelf_stocking/design_docs/cracker_box_pick_drop_collection.md)). A USB 3 device on a SuperSpeed-capable port that comes up at USB 2 points to the cable, an extension, or a half-seated USB-C plug.
- **`shelf_cam` matches this convention by configuration.** The ZED topic names and its millimetre depth payload are coupled: change one only with the other ([`zed_shelf_cam.md`](zed_shelf_cam.md)).
- **Don't rename through Groot** — the name appears in many places (MJCF, URDF, BT XMLs, YAMLs, C++ defaults). Use grep across the whole workspace if you ever need to do this again.
